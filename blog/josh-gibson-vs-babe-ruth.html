Josh Gibson vs. Babe Ruth: who was the better hitter?

BaseRogue Blog · 2026-11-18

Two of the greatest sluggers who ever lived, side by side: the numbers, the legends and how they compare when you put them on the same field.

The short answer

On a per-at-bat basis, Josh Gibson and Babe Ruth are as close as two hitters can be. Since Major League Baseball folded the Negro Leagues of 1920–1948 into its official record in 2024, Gibson owns the highest career batting average (.372), slugging percentage (.718) and OPS (1.177) in major league history. Ruth is second in OPS at 1.164. The difference is not talent but volume: Ruth played 22 seasons of 150-game schedules; Gibson played in leagues that scheduled a fraction of that, and he died at 35 in 1947, months before Jackie Robinson's debut.

Gibson hit for a higher average and the same slugging. Ruth walked far more, so he got on base more often and hit home runs a bit more often per at-bat. Gibson did all of that as a catcher, the hardest defensive position on the field, while Ruth played the outfield.

The legend of 800 home runs

You will often read that Gibson hit "almost 800" or even "nearly 1,000" home runs. Those totals count barnstorming tours, exhibitions and winter ball against every level of competition, the same kind of games that are not counted for Ruth either. In documented league games, the number is about 200 home runs in under 3,000 at-bats. That is still a pace of more than 40 home runs a season, and his 1943 batting average of .466 is now the highest single-season mark in major league history.

So who was better?

Ruth had the longer, fuller career against the best competition he was allowed to face, and he changed the game more than any player before or since. Gibson put up the same rate of production as a catcher, in shorter seasons, without ever being allowed into the major leagues of his day. On the field, inning for inning, the honest answer is that they were equals. Off it, only one of them got the chance to prove it every day.

FAQ

Who has the highest career batting average in MLB history?
Since 2024, Josh Gibson, at .372, after Major League Baseball added the Negro Leagues of 1920-1948 to its official records. Ty Cobb (.367) is second.
Did Josh Gibson really hit 800 home runs?
Not in official games. Totals of 800 or more include barnstorming, exhibitions and winter ball. In documented Negro League games he hit about 200 home runs in fewer than 3,000 at-bats.
Who has the higher OPS, Josh Gibson or Babe Ruth?
Josh Gibson, 1.177 to Babe Ruth's 1.164, in MLB's official records. Ruth has far more plate appearances.
